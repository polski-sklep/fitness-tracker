# About Jacob's Fitness Tracker

Jacob's Fitness Tracker is a single-user training log for strength sessions and cycling rides.

## What it tracks
- Strength sessions parsed from Telegram text or screenshots
- Cycling rides with distance, duration, calories, watts, and cadence
- Exercise history, estimated 1RM, PR markers, and muscle-group volume
- Dashboard trends for Power Index, cycling calories, recent sessions, and weekly muscle-group load

## What Power Index means
Power Index is the strength-training headline score. It answers one question: “How much useful strength work did this session represent compared with Jacob’s own normal training?”

A score of 100 means the session landed around Jacob’s personal median baseline. Scores above 100 mean the session produced more work than usual. Scores below 100 mean it produced less work than usual. The score is personal, not universal: a 140 for Jacob means “strong relative to Jacob,” not “better than someone else.”

The score is built from the logged strength work in the session:

- Exercise volume: sets, reps, and load
- Exercise type: heavy compounds count more than isolation or corrective work
- Relative intensity: how heavy the work was compared with Jacob’s best estimated 1RM for that exercise
- Effort: how close the sets were to failure, using reps-in-reserve
- Sensitivity: joint or tendon irritation can reduce credit when an exercise aggravates something
- Readiness and quality: the same output can mean different things on a low-readiness day versus a high-readiness day
- Time efficiency: useful work done in a reasonable time scores better than the same work spread out inefficiently

Power Index is useful for spotting patterns. It helps separate a genuinely strong session from a long but unfocused one, and it makes it easier to see whether recent training is trending above or below baseline. It is not a scientific recovery model or a medical signal. It is a practical personal index for comparing Jacob’s sessions against Jacob’s own history.

## What cycling uses
Cycling does not use Power Index. Calories are the headline metric, with ride details available for context.
