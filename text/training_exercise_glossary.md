# Training Exercise Glossary

## 1. Basic notation

- **LEGS / PUSH / PULL**: Workout split. Legs train lower body. Push trains chest, shoulders, triceps. Pull trains back, biceps, rear shoulders.

- **kg × reps × sets**: Load, repetitions, sets. Example: **60kg × 12 × 3** means 3 sets of 12 reps using 60kg.

- **L3 / L8**: Machine or cable stack level. This is not universal weight. It only means level 3 or level 8 on that specific machine.

- **RIR**: Reps in reserve. How many more clean reps you could have done before failure. Example: **RIR 2** means you stopped with roughly 2 reps left.

- **Failure**: You cannot complete another clean rep with good form.

- **BW**: Bodyweight. No added weight.

- **DB**: Dumbbell.

- **Neutral grip**: Palms face each other. Usually easier on wrists, elbows, and shoulders.

- **Int / ext rotation**: Internal and external shoulder rotation. Used for rotator cuff warm-up and shoulder injury prevention.

---

## 2. Session scoring categories

- **Readiness**: How prepared you were before training. Sleep, food, hydration, stress, motivation, soreness, alcohol, and supplements.

- **Sensitivity**: Injury-risk score. Tracks pain, joint irritation, tendon discomfort, tightness, or warning signs.

- **Fatigue**: How much the workout taxed you. Includes muscle fatigue, breathlessness, recovery between sets, soreness, and how close to failure you trained.

- **Quality**: Execution score. Form, control, tempo, posture, range of motion, focus, and whether you followed the plan properly.

- **Time**: Total workout duration. Used to judge training density and efficiency. Long is not automatically better.

## 3. Warm-up, mobility, and core

- **[Full body stretch](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=full+body+stretch+routine)**: General mobility work before lifting. Should be dynamic at the start, not long passive holds.

- **[Rotator cuff external rotation band](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=band+external+rotation+shoulder+exercise)**: Band exercise for the small shoulder stabilizers. Helps protect shoulders before pressing, pulling, and overhead work.

- **[Rotator cuff internal rotation band](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=band+internal+rotation+shoulder+exercise)**: Similar shoulder prehab, but rotates the arm inward. Useful but should be controlled and light.

- **[Cable woodchoppers](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=cable+woodchopper+exercise)**: Rotational core exercise using a cable machine. Trains obliques, trunk control, and twisting strength.

- **[Hanging leg raises](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=hanging+leg+raise+exercise)**: Core exercise hanging from a bar. Mainly trains lower abs and hip flexors. Also taxes grip and shoulders.

- **[Sit-ups](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=sit+up+exercise)**: Trunk flexion exercise. Works abs, but can irritate lower back if rushed or overdone.

- **[Crunches](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=crunch+exercise+abs)**: Shorter version of sit-ups. More isolated ab work with less hip flexor involvement.

- **[Butterfly kicks](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=butterfly+kicks+abs+exercise)**: Flutter-kick style ab exercise. Trains lower abs and hip flexors.

- **[Lying leg raise](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=lying+leg+raise+exercise)**: Core movement lying on your back, raising legs. Trains lower abs and hip flexors.

- **[Mountain climbers](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=mountain+climber+exercise)**: Dynamic core and conditioning movement. Also raises heart rate.

- **[Thread the needle plank](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=thread+the+needle+plank+exercise)**: Rotational plank variation. Trains obliques, shoulder stability, and trunk control.

- **[Plank](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=plank+exercise)**: Static core hold. Trains abs, deep core, glutes, and posture.

## 4. Leg exercises

- **[Back squat](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=barbell+back+squat+exercise)**: Barbell squat with the bar on your upper back. Main lower-body strength movement. Trains quads, glutes, hamstrings, core, and back stability.

- **[Barbell squat](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=barbell+squat+exercise)**: Same general category as back squat unless specified otherwise. If both are listed, they are likely duplicates unless one is a warm-up or different variation.

- **[Squat machine](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=squat+machine+exercise)**: Machine-based squat pattern. More guided and stable than barbell squats. Usually less balance demand.

- **[Romanian deadlift](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=romanian+deadlift+exercise)**: Hip-hinge exercise. Main target is hamstrings and glutes. Keep spine neutral and feel the stretch in hamstrings.

- **[Leg press](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=leg+press+machine+exercise)**: Machine press using legs. Trains quads, glutes, and hamstrings with less spinal loading than squats.

- **[Leg extensions](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=leg+extension+machine+exercise)**: Machine isolation for quads. Good for front-thigh hypertrophy. Can stress knees if too heavy or jerky.

- **[Leg curl](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=leg+curl+machine+exercise)**: Hamstring isolation machine. Usually lying or seated depending on machine.

- **[Seated hamstring curl](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=seated+hamstring+curl+machine+exercise)**: Hamstring curl done seated. Often very effective because it loads hamstrings in a lengthened position.

- **[Abductor](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=hip+abductor+machine+exercise)**: Machine where knees move outward. Trains outer hips and glute medius. Useful for hip stability.

- **[Lower body stretch + roller](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=lower+body+stretching+foam+roller)**: Cool-down using static stretching and foam rolling. Targets quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves, and hips.

## 5. Push exercises

- **[Flat barbell bench press](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=flat+barbell+bench+press+exercise)**: Main chest press with a barbell. Trains chest, front shoulders, and triceps.

- **[Incline DB press neutral grip](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=neutral+grip+incline+dumbbell+press)**: Dumbbell press on an incline bench with palms facing each other. Targets upper chest and is usually shoulder-friendlier than barbell incline.

- **[Machine chest press](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=machine+chest+press+exercise)**: Guided chest press. Easier to control than free weights and useful for safe high-volume chest work.

- **[Machine overhead press](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=machine+overhead+press+exercise)**: Shoulder press machine. Trains shoulders and triceps. Can irritate shoulders if range or load is wrong.

- **[Dips to failure](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=bodyweight+dips+exercise)**: Bodyweight pressing movement. Trains chest, triceps, and front shoulders. High stress on shoulders, elbows, and sternum if form or fatigue is poor.

- **[Cable chest fly](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=cable+chest+fly+exercise)**: Cable isolation for chest. Trains the pecs through a wide arc. Should be controlled, not yanked.

- **[Pec fly machine](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=pec+deck+fly+machine+exercise)**: Machine chest fly. Similar to cable fly but more fixed. Good for chest isolation.

- **[Machine reverse pec deck flies](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=reverse+pec+deck+fly+exercise)**: Rear-delt exercise done on the pec deck facing the opposite way. Trains rear shoulders and upper back stability.

- **[Cable tricep pushdown](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=cable+tricep+pushdown+exercise)**: Triceps isolation using cable, pushing down from high pulley.

- **[Cable tricep pulldown](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=cable+tricep+pulldown+exercise)**: Usually the same as pushdown unless your gym uses different attachments or naming.

## 6. Pull exercises

- **[Neutral grip pull-ups to failure](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=neutral+grip+pull+up+exercise)**: Bodyweight pull-up with palms facing each other. Trains lats, upper back, biceps, grip. Neutral grip is usually joint-friendlier.

- **[Dumbbell row](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=dumbbell+row+exercise)**: One-arm or chest-supported row with dumbbells. Trains lats, upper back, and biceps.

- **[Machine row](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=machine+row+exercise)**: Guided row machine. Trains mid-back, lats, rhomboids, and rear delts.

- **[Neutral grip lat pulldown](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=neutral+grip+lat+pulldown+exercise)**: Cable pulldown with palms facing each other. Trains lats and biceps with lower shoulder stress.

- **[Lateral pulldown](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=lat+pulldown+exercise)**: Likely means lat pulldown. Pulling a bar or handles down to train the lats.

- **[Machine pulldown](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=machine+lat+pulldown+exercise)**: Machine version of lat pulldown. More fixed and stable.

- **[Seated cable row](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=seated+cable+row+exercise)**: Cable row from seated position. Trains mid-back, lats, rear delts, and biceps.

- **[Bicep curls](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=dumbbell+bicep+curl+exercise)**: Isolation exercise for biceps. Also stresses elbow tendons if overloaded or taken to failure too often.

- **[Face pulls](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=face+pull+exercise)**: Cable exercise pulling toward face. Trains rear delts, traps, rotator cuff, and shoulder stability.

- **[Tricep pulldown on pull day](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=cable+tricep+pulldown+exercise)**: Triceps movement placed in a pull session. Not wrong, but it belongs more naturally on push day unless used lightly for extra arm volume.
